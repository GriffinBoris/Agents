# Strict Controlled Writing

Apply these additions only to procedures, runbooks, safety text, terse user-facing messages, or an explicit Simplified Technical English request. Apply the plain technical-writing rules first.

## Strict Rules

- Limit an instruction to 20 words and descriptive text to 25 words when the limit does not remove meaning. Split a longer sentence or flag why it must stay intact.
- Use simple present, simple past, simple future, infinitive, or imperative forms. Avoid perfect tenses and stacked auxiliary verbs.
- Do not use contractions or semicolons.
- Prefer one-word action verbs over phrasal verbs and `-ing` main-verb constructions.
- Keep a multi-word noun to three words when possible. Rewrite a longer noun cluster to show the relationship between its parts.
- Use an article or demonstrative before a specific countable noun when English requires one. Do not add articles to labels or general statements merely to satisfy a pattern.
- Keep a paragraph to one topic and no more than six sentences when practical.
- Use American spelling unless the repository or requested artifact requires another spelling convention.
- Do not ban em dashes under the name of STE; the STE punctuation rule targets semicolons. Apply a separate house-style ban only when one exists.

## Controlled Choices

- Use `because` for cause when `since` could be read as time.
- Use `can` for ability or permission. Use `must` only for a real obligation.
- Prefer `but` to `however` and `use` or `with` to an introductory `using` phrase when the rewrite stays natural.
- Prefer `make sure` to `ensure`, and a direct action verb to `perform` plus a noun.
- Do not apply a mechanical substitution when it changes the original meaning.

## Procedures and Safety Text

- Put each procedural action in a numbered step and begin it with a command.
- Put the condition before its command and separate the two with a comma.
- Use `WARNING` for risk of injury, `CAUTION` for risk of damage, and `NOTE` for information only.
- Put a warning or caution immediately before the step it protects.
- Do not put an instruction inside a note.

## Strict Review Checklist

1. Is any instruction longer than 20 words or other sentence longer than 25 words?
2. Does the text contain a contraction, semicolon, perfect tense, or stacked auxiliary?
3. Can a known actor replace passive voice?
4. Can a direct verb replace a nominalization, phrasal verb, or `-ing` construction?
5. Does any noun cluster contain four or more words?
6. Does every ordered step contain one primary action?
7. Is each safety label accurate and placed next to the protected step?
